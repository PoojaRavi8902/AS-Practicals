LAN and WAN (Local Area Network vs Wide Area Network)

1. Definition of LAN

A Local Area Network (LAN) is a network that connects computers and devices within a limited geographical area such as:

  • A single building
  • A school campus
  • An office block
  • A factory site

A LAN is usually owned and managed by a single organisation.

2. Definition of WAN

A Wide Area Network (WAN) connects computers or entire LANs over a large geographical area such as:

  • Between cities
  • Between countries
  • Globally

The Internet is the largest WAN in existence.

3. Key Differences Between LAN and WAN

Feature LAN WAN
Geographical Area Small Large
Ownership Private Uses public infrastructure
Speed High Lower than LAN
Latency Low Higher

4. Bandwidth and Latency Explained

Bandwidth is the amount of data that can be transmitted per second.

Latency is the delay before data transfer begins following a request.

LANs typically have high bandwidth and low latency because devices are close together. WANs have lower bandwidth and higher latency due to long-distance transmission.

5. Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages of LAN

  • High speed
  • Easy file sharing
  • Centralised backup
  • Low communication cost

Disadvantages of LAN

  • Limited coverage area
  • Requires maintenance

Advantages of WAN

  • Global communication
  • Remote access
  • Supports international business

Disadvantages of WAN

  • Higher latency
  • More security risks
  • Dependence on service providers

6. Exam-Style 4-Mark Answer Example

Question: Explain two differences between a LAN and a WAN. [4]

Model Answer:

A LAN connects devices within a small geographical area such as a school or office, whereas a WAN connects networks across large geographical areas such as cities or countries. Additionally, LANs are typically privately owned and provide higher speeds, while WANs rely on public infrastructure and have higher latency.

7. Real-World Scenario

A company with multiple office buildings on one site would use a LAN. If it connects its offices across different countries, it would use a WAN.

Network Topologies

A network topology describes the physical or logical arrangement of devices and communication links within a network.

Understanding topology is crucial because it affects:

  • Performance
  • Fault tolerance
  • Scalability
  • Cost
  • Maintenance

1. Bus Topology

In a bus topology, all devices share a single backbone cable.

Advantages

  • Cheap to install
  • Uses less cable
  • Simple design

Disadvantages

  • If backbone fails, entire network fails
  • Difficult fault detection
  • Performance decreases as devices increase

2. Star Topology

All devices connect to a central hub or switch.

Advantages

  • Easy fault detection
  • Failure of one device does not affect others
  • High performance

Disadvantages

  • Central device failure stops entire network
  • More cabling required

3. Ring Topology

Each device connects to exactly two others, forming a circular path.

Advantages

  • Predictable performance
  • No collisions (if token-based)

Disadvantages

  • Failure of one device may affect entire ring
  • Difficult to reconfigure

4. Mesh Topology

Each device connects to multiple other devices.

Advantages

  • Very reliable
  • No single point of failure
  • High redundancy

Disadvantages

  • Expensive
  • Complex cabling

5. Hybrid Topology

A hybrid topology combines two or more topologies. Example: Star-Bus network.

6. Comparison Table

Topology Fault Tolerance Cost Scalability
Bus Low Low Limited
Star Medium Medium High
Mesh Very High High Moderate

7. 6-Mark Evaluation Example

Question: Evaluate the suitability of star topology for a school network. [6]

Model Answer:

Star topology is suitable for a school because failure of one device does not affect the rest of the network. It allows easy addition of new computers, making it scalable. However, if the central switch fails, the entire network stops functioning. Despite this, star topology is widely used in schools due to ease of maintenance and reliability.

Transmission Media and Network Hardware

1. Transmission Media

Transmission media refers to the physical or wireless pathway through which data travels between devices.

A. Twisted Pair Cable (Copper)

Twisted pair cable consists of pairs of copper wires twisted together to reduce electromagnetic interference.

Advantages:
  • Cheap
  • Easy to install
  • Widely used in LANs
Disadvantages:
  • Limited to ~100m
  • Susceptible to interference
  • Lower bandwidth than fibre

B. Coaxial Cable

Coaxial cable has a central conductor surrounded by insulation and shielding.

Advantages:
  • Better shielding than twisted pair
  • Higher bandwidth
Disadvantages:
  • More expensive
  • Less flexible

C. Fibre Optic Cable

Fibre optic cable transmits data as pulses of light.

Advantages:
  • Very high bandwidth
  • Long distance transmission
  • Immune to electromagnetic interference
  • More secure
Disadvantages:
  • Expensive
  • Fragile
  • Requires specialist installation

D. Wireless Transmission

Wireless networks use radio waves or microwaves to transmit data.

Advantages:
  • Mobility
  • Easy installation
  • No physical cables
Disadvantages:
  • Security risks
  • Interference
  • Lower speed than fibre

2. Bandwidth and Interference

Bandwidth refers to how much data can be transmitted per second. Measured in bits per second (bps).

Interference occurs when unwanted signals disrupt data transmission. Copper cables are more susceptible than fibre.

3. Network Hardware Devices

Router

A router connects different networks and forwards data packets using IP addresses.

Switch

A switch connects devices within a LAN and forwards frames using MAC addresses.

NIC (Network Interface Card)

A NIC enables a device to connect to a network. Each NIC has a unique MAC address.

Wireless Access Point (WAP)

A WAP allows wireless devices to connect to a wired network.

Modem

A modem converts digital signals to analogue signals (and vice versa) for transmission over telephone lines.

Firewall

A firewall monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on security rules.

IP Addressing – Deep Dive

IP stands for Internet Protocol. An IP address uniquely identifies a device on a network.

1. IPv4 Structure

IPv4 uses a 32-bit binary number divided into four 8-bit sections (octets). It is written in dotted decimal notation.

Example: 205.124.16.152

2. Dotted Decimal ↔ Binary Example

205 = 11001101 124 = 01111100 16 = 00010000 152 = 10011000

4. Classful Addressing

Class A → first bit = 0 Class B → first two bits = 10 Class C → first three bits = 110

205 in binary begins with 110 → Therefore Class C.

5. NetID and HostID

An IP address consists of:

  • NetID – identifies the network
  • HostID – identifies the device

6. CIDR Notation

CIDR (Classless Inter-Domain Routing) uses slash notation.

205.124.16.152/24

/24 means first 24 bits are network bits. Remaining 8 bits = host bits.

7. Private vs Public IP Addresses

Private ranges:

  • 10.0.0.0 – 10.255.255.255
  • 172.16.0.0 – 172.31.255.255
  • 192.168.0.0 – 192.168.255.255

Private addresses are used within local networks. Public addresses are used on the Internet.

8. NAT (Network Address Translation)

NAT translates private IP addresses into public IP addresses.

9. Exam-Style 4-Mark Example

Question: Explain why an IPv4 address defines a NetID and a HostID. [4]

The NetID identifies the network to which a device belongs, while the HostID identifies the specific device within that network. This allows routers to route data between networks while switches identify devices within a local network.

10. 6-Mark Evaluation

Question: Evaluate the need for CIDR. [6]

CIDR improves IP address allocation efficiency by allowing flexible subnetting. It reduces waste compared to classful addressing. However, it requires careful network planning. Overall, CIDR allows better scalability of the Internet.

Domain Name System (DNS) and URL Resolution

1. What is DNS?

The Domain Name System (DNS) translates human-readable domain names (e.g., www.example.com) into IP addresses.

DNS software runs on dedicated DNS servers. These servers are specialised hardware machines distributed globally.

2. URL Breakdown

Example URL:

https://www.schoolsite.org/resources/video.mp4

  • https → Protocol
  • www.schoolsite.org → Domain name
  • /resources/video.mp4 → Resource path

3. DNS Resolution Process (Step-by-Step)

  1. User enters URL in browser
  2. Browser checks local cache
  3. If not found, query sent to local DNS resolver
  4. Resolver queries root server
  5. Root refers to TLD server (.org, .com)
  6. TLD refers to authoritative server
  7. Authoritative server returns IP address
  8. Browser connects to IP address

6. Streaming Technologies

On-Demand Bit Streaming

Video is divided into small packets and streamed continuously. The video begins playing before the entire file downloads.

7. Buffering Explained

Buffering stores a small portion of the video temporarily to prevent interruptions if network speed fluctuates.

8. Measures to Prevent Video Pausing

  • High bandwidth connection
  • Low latency
  • Content Delivery Networks (CDNs)
  • Adaptive bitrate streaming
  • Sufficient buffer size

9. 4-Mark Exam Example

Question: Explain how a URL and DNS are used to locate a resource. [4]

The URL specifies the protocol and domain name. The DNS translates the domain name into an IP address. The browser then uses the IP address to request the resource from the server.

10. 6-Mark Streaming Question Example

Question: Describe measures needed to ensure video does not pause. [6]

A stable high-bandwidth connection is required. Buffering stores part of the video locally. Adaptive bitrate streaming adjusts quality based on speed. CDNs reduce distance between server and user. These measures reduce latency and packet loss.

Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN)

1. Definition

The Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) is the traditional circuit-switched telephone network used worldwide for voice communication.

It connects telephones using dedicated physical circuits established for the duration of a call.

2. Circuit Switching Explained

Circuit switching establishes a dedicated communication path between two devices before data transmission begins.

  • A connection is set up
  • The path remains reserved
  • Data flows continuously
  • The circuit is released after communication ends

3. PSTN as a Network Option for a Company

A company could use PSTN in the following ways:

  • Dial-up Internet access
  • Remote branch communication
  • Voice communication between buildings

However, PSTN provides lower bandwidth compared to modern broadband fibre connections.

4. Circuit Switching vs Packet Switching

Feature Circuit Switching Packet Switching
Connection Type Dedicated Shared
Efficiency Lower Higher
Used In Traditional telephone calls Internet

5. 4-Mark Exam Example

Question: Define what a PSTN is or does. [2]

The Public Switched Telephone Network is a circuit-switched telephone network that establishes a dedicated communication path between callers for the duration of a call.

6. 4-Mark Explanation Example

Question: Explain how a PSTN could provide a network for a company. [4]

A PSTN can connect remote offices using telephone lines. It establishes dedicated circuits between locations. Data or voice can be transmitted over these circuits. However, bandwidth may be limited compared to fibre-based WANs.
