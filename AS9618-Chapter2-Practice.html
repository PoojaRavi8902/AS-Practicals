LAN & WAN Practice

1. Define a LAN. [2]

A LAN is a network that connects devices within a limited geographical area such as a building or campus.

2. Define a WAN. [2]

A WAN is a network that connects devices or LANs across large geographical areas such as cities or countries.

3. State one advantage of a LAN. [1]

High data transfer speed.

4. State one disadvantage of a WAN. [1]

Higher latency compared to LAN.

5. Explain two differences between LAN and WAN. [4]

LAN covers a small geographical area and is privately owned, whereas WAN covers large areas and often uses public infrastructure. LAN typically offers higher speeds and lower latency than WAN.

6. Explain why latency is generally higher in a WAN. [3]

Data must travel longer distances and pass through multiple routers and networks, increasing delay.

7. A company has three buildings on one campus. Which network type is suitable and why? [3]

A LAN is suitable because the buildings are within a limited geographical area and require high-speed internal communication.

8. A company operates offices in different countries. Which network type is required? [2]

A WAN is required because the offices are geographically separated.

9. Explain the term bandwidth. [2]

Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data that can be transmitted per second, usually measured in bits per second.

10. Explain the term latency. [2]

Latency is the delay before data begins transferring after a request.

11. Explain two advantages of using a WAN for business. [4]

WAN allows communication between remote offices and enables global collaboration. It supports remote access to shared resources.

12. Identify two factors other than cost when choosing network infrastructure. [2]

Bandwidth requirements and security.

13. Describe how a WAN can connect multiple LANs. [4]

Each LAN connects to a router. Routers connect through public or private communication lines forming a WAN.

14. Explain why LANs typically have lower error rates than WANs. [3]

LANs use shorter cables and fewer intermediate devices, reducing interference and transmission errors.

15. Give two security concerns associated with WANs. [2]

Data interception and unauthorised access.

16. Explain why a LAN may be more secure than a WAN. [3]

LANs are privately controlled and not exposed directly to public networks.

17. What is meant by privately owned network infrastructure? [2]

The network hardware and cables are owned and managed by the organisation itself.

18. State one reason why WANs are more expensive to maintain. [1]

They rely on third-party service providers.

19. Evaluate whether a small school needs a WAN. [4]

A small school on one site likely only needs a LAN. A WAN is only necessary if connecting multiple campuses.

20. Describe how fibre optic cable improves WAN performance. [3]

Fibre offers higher bandwidth, longer transmission distance and immunity to interference.

21. Explain the term packet switching. [2]

Data is divided into packets and transmitted independently across a shared network.

22. Explain why packet switching is efficient for WAN communication. [3]

It allows multiple users to share network paths, improving utilisation and reducing wasted bandwidth.

23. Compare circuit switching and packet switching. [4]

Circuit switching establishes a dedicated path, while packet switching shares network resources. Packet switching is more efficient for data communication.

24. Suggest a suitable network type for a hospital with multiple city branches. [3]

A WAN is suitable because it connects multiple geographically separated sites.

25. Evaluate the suitability of a WAN for international banking systems. [6]

WAN is essential for international banking to allow secure communication between branches globally. However, high security measures and redundancy are required to ensure reliability.

Network Topologies Practice

1. Define network topology. [2]

Network topology is the physical or logical arrangement of devices and connections in a network.

2. Describe a bus topology. [3]

In a bus topology, all devices are connected to a single backbone cable. Data travels along this cable and is received by all devices.

3. State one advantage of a bus topology. [1]

Uses less cabling compared to other topologies.

4. State one disadvantage of a bus topology. [1]

If the main cable fails, the whole network fails.

5. Describe a star topology. [3]

In a star topology, all devices are connected to a central hub or switch. Communication passes through this central device.

6. Explain one advantage of star topology over bus. [2]

If one cable fails, only one device is affected, unlike bus where entire network may fail.

7. Explain one disadvantage of star topology. [2]

If the central hub or switch fails, the entire network stops functioning.

8. Describe a ring topology. [3]

In a ring topology, devices are connected in a circular structure, where each device connects to two others forming a closed loop.

9. Explain how data travels in a ring topology. [3]

Data travels in one direction around the ring, passing through each device until it reaches its destination.

10. State one disadvantage of ring topology. [1]

A break in the ring can disrupt the entire network.

11. Describe a mesh topology. [3]

In a mesh topology, devices are interconnected with multiple paths between nodes, providing redundancy.

12. Explain why mesh topology is highly reliable. [3]

Multiple paths exist between devices, so if one link fails, data can take an alternative route.

13. State one disadvantage of mesh topology. [1]

Requires a large amount of cabling.

14. Define hybrid topology. [2]

A hybrid topology combines two or more different network topologies.

15. Explain why hybrid topology is used in large organisations. [3]

It allows flexibility by combining advantages of different topologies for different areas of the organisation.

16. Compare star and mesh topology in terms of cost. [3]

Star topology is cheaper due to less cabling. Mesh is expensive because of multiple connections between nodes.

17. Compare bus and star topology in terms of fault tolerance. [3]

Bus has low fault tolerance since backbone failure stops network. Star isolates failures to individual devices.

18. A small classroom network needs low cost and simple installation. Suggest a suitable topology. [2]

Star topology is suitable due to ease of installation and manageable cost.

19. A military communication system requires maximum reliability. Which topology is best? [2]

Mesh topology due to redundancy and fault tolerance.

20. Explain scalability in network topologies. [2]

Scalability refers to how easily devices can be added to the network without major disruption.

21. Which topology is easiest to expand? Explain. [3]

Star topology, because new devices can connect directly to the central switch.

22. Explain how traffic collisions occur in a bus topology. [3]

If two devices transmit at the same time, signals interfere causing collision and data corruption.

23. Why are switches preferred over hubs in star topology? [2]

Switches send data only to the intended device, reducing collisions.

24. Evaluate the suitability of bus topology for a modern office. [4]

Bus is inexpensive but unreliable and not scalable, making it unsuitable for modern office environments.

25. Evaluate the suitability of mesh topology for a data centre. [6]

Mesh provides redundancy and high reliability, essential for data centres. However, it is expensive and complex to maintain.

26. Describe how a token works in a ring topology. [3]

A token circulates around the ring; only the device holding the token can transmit data.

27. Explain why bus topology is rarely used today. [3]

It has low fault tolerance, limited scalability, and higher risk of collisions.

28. Identify one topology commonly used in home networks. [1]

Star topology.

29. Suggest a hybrid topology combining star and mesh and explain its benefit. [4]

Multiple star networks interconnected in mesh form improve reliability while controlling cost.

30. Discuss how topology affects network performance. [6]

Topology influences data flow, collision risk, fault tolerance and scalability. Star reduces collisions, mesh improves reliability, while bus may suffer congestion.

IP Addressing Monster Bank

1. What does IP stand for? [1]

Internet Protocol.

2. What is an IPv4 address? [2]

A 32-bit binary address used to uniquely identify devices on a network.

3. How many bits are in IPv4? [1]

32 bits.

4. How many octets are in IPv4? [1]

4 octets.

5. What is dotted decimal notation? [2]

A way of writing 32-bit IPv4 addresses as four decimal numbers separated by dots.

6. Convert 192 to binary. [2]

11000000

7. Convert 255 to binary. [2]

11111111

8. Convert 128 to binary. [2]

10000000

9. Convert 11001101 to decimal. [2]

205

10. Convert 11111100 to decimal. [2]

252

11. Is 256.12.5.1 valid? Give reason. [2]

Invalid because each octet must be between 0 and 255.

12. Is 192.168.1.300 valid? [2]

Invalid because 300 exceeds maximum 255.

13. Is 10.0.0.1 valid? [1]

Yes, valid IPv4 address.

14. Identify class of 10.5.3.1. [2]

Class A (first bit 0).

15. Identify class of 172.16.4.5. [2]

Class B.

16. Identify class of 205.124.16.152. [2]

Class C (first three bits 110).

17. Explain why IPv4 defines NetID and HostID. [3]

NetID identifies network, HostID identifies specific device within that network.

18. What does /24 mean in CIDR notation? [2]

First 24 bits represent network portion.

19. In 192.168.1.5/24, how many host bits? [2]

8 host bits (32 - 24).

20. In 10.0.0.0/8, how many network bits? [2]

8 network bits.

21. Calculate number of hosts in /30 network. [3]

2 host bits → 2² = 4 addresses.

22. State one private IP range. [1]

192.168.0.0 – 192.168.255.255

23. Explain difference between public and private IP. [3]

Public IP is globally routable. Private IP is used within local networks.

24. Why is NAT required? [3]

NAT translates private IP addresses into public IP addresses for Internet communication.

25. A device has IP 192.168.1.10. Is this public or private? [2]

Private IP.

26. Explain how routers use IP addresses. [3]

Routers read destination IP address to determine where to forward packets.

27. Describe why IPv4 addresses are running out. [3]

Limited 32-bit address space leads to address exhaustion.

28. What is IPv6? [2]

128-bit IP addressing system.

29. Compare IPv4 and IPv6. [4]

IPv4 uses 32 bits; IPv6 uses 128 bits providing much larger address space.

30. Evaluate benefits of CIDR over classful addressing. [6]

CIDR allows flexible subnetting, reduces waste, improves efficiency of IP allocation.

31. Explain subnetting. [3]

Subnetting divides a network into smaller sub-networks by borrowing host bits.

32. Why is subnetting useful? [3]

Improves network organisation, reduces broadcast traffic, increases security.

33. What is a default gateway? [2]

Device that forwards traffic from local network to other networks.

34. Explain broadcast address. [2]

Special address used to send data to all devices in network.

35. Explain loopback address. [2]

127.0.0.1 used for testing local machine.

36. Discuss advantages of private addressing. [4]

Improves security and conserves public IP addresses.

37. Discuss limitations of NAT. [4]

Can complicate peer-to-peer communication and adds processing overhead.

38. Calculate total addresses in /16 network. [3]

16 host bits → 2^16 = 65,536 addresses.

39. Identify host bits in 205.124.16.152/24. [2]

Last 8 bits represent host.

40. Explain importance of IP addressing in routing. [4]

Routers use IP addresses to determine packet destination and optimal path.

41. Define subnet mask. [2]

A 32-bit number used to separate network and host portions.

42. Write subnet mask for /24. [1]

255.255.255.0

43. Write subnet mask for /16. [1]

255.255.0.0

44. Explain role of DHCP. [3]

DHCP automatically assigns IP addresses to devices.

45. Explain static vs dynamic IP. [3]

Static IP is manually configured. Dynamic IP is automatically assigned by DHCP.

46. Why are IP addresses hierarchical? [3]

To allow efficient routing by dividing into network and host portions.

47. Explain why routers do not use MAC addresses for routing across networks. [3]

MAC addresses operate within LAN; IP addresses identify networks globally.

48. Describe role of ARP. [3]

ARP translates IP addresses into MAC addresses within a LAN.

49. Evaluate IPv6 as a long-term solution. [6]

IPv6 provides massive address space and improved routing efficiency, solving IPv4 exhaustion.

50. Discuss challenges of IPv6 adoption. [4]

Requires hardware upgrades and dual-stack implementation.

51. Explain difference between unicast, multicast and broadcast. [3]

Unicast is one-to-one, multicast is one-to-many selected, broadcast is one-to-all.

52. What is CIDR mainly designed to improve? [2]

Efficient allocation of IP addresses.

53. Why is 127.0.0.1 reserved? [2]

It is the loopback address for local testing.

54. Explain why broadcast traffic should be limited. [3]

Too much broadcast traffic reduces network performance.

55. Evaluate the importance of IP addressing in modern networking. [6]

IP addressing enables global routing, device identification, scalability and Internet communication.

DNS, URL & Streaming Monster Bank

1. What does DNS stand for? [1]

Domain Name System.

2. What is the main purpose of DNS? [2]

DNS translates domain names into IP addresses.

3. Identify the type of software used by DNS and the hardware it runs on. [2]

DNS server software running on a DNS server (specialised server hardware).

4. Explain why DNS is necessary. [3]

Humans use domain names; computers communicate using IP addresses. DNS bridges this gap.

5. Describe the process when a user enters a URL into a browser. [4]

Browser queries DNS server, DNS resolves domain to IP address, browser connects to server using IP, resource is retrieved.

6. What is a recursive DNS query? [2]

DNS server fully resolves the query on behalf of the client.

7. What is an iterative DNS query? [2]

DNS server returns the best answer it has, referring client to another server if necessary.

8. Explain caching in DNS. [3]

DNS stores resolved addresses temporarily to reduce future lookup time.

9. Explain how DNS improves Internet efficiency. [3]

Caching reduces repeated lookups, decreasing latency and network traffic.

10. What happens if DNS fails? [2]

Users cannot access websites using domain names.

11. Define URL. [2]

Uniform Resource Locator; it specifies the address of a resource on the Internet.

12. Identify components of https://www.example.com/index.html [3]

Protocol (https), domain name (www.example.com), file path (index.html).

13. Explain role of HTTPS. [3]

HTTPS encrypts data between browser and server for security.

14. Explain difference between domain name and IP address. [2]

Domain name is human-readable; IP address is numeric machine-readable.

15. Define streaming. [2]

Streaming is continuous transmission of media data allowing playback before download completes.

16. What is on-demand streaming? [2]

User selects content and it is streamed when requested.

17. What is live streaming? [2]

Real-time broadcast of content over the Internet.

18. Explain buffering. [3]

Temporary storage of video data before playback to prevent interruptions.

19. Why does buffering reduce pauses? [3]

Ensures enough data is stored before playback continues.

20. What is adaptive bitrate streaming? [3]

Streaming adjusts video quality based on available bandwidth.

21. Explain why adaptive streaming improves user experience. [3]

Prevents buffering by lowering resolution when bandwidth drops.

22. What is a CDN? [2]

Content Delivery Network; distributed servers delivering content closer to users.

23. Explain how CDNs reduce latency. [3]

Content is delivered from geographically closer servers.

24. Why are CDNs important for streaming platforms? [3]

They handle high traffic and ensure fast, reliable delivery.

25. A video keeps pausing while streaming. Suggest causes. [4]

Low bandwidth, high latency, network congestion, insufficient buffering.

26. Suggest measures to prevent streaming interruptions. [6]

High-speed connection, buffering, adaptive bitrate streaming, CDNs, low latency, QoS prioritisation.

27. Explain DNS hierarchy. [4]

Root servers → TLD servers → Authoritative name servers.

28. What is an authoritative DNS server? [2]

Server that holds official DNS records for a domain.

29. Explain DNS spoofing. [3]

Attack where false DNS data redirects users to malicious sites.

30. Explain how DNSSEC improves security. [3]

Adds cryptographic signatures to verify DNS responses.

31. Evaluate importance of DNS for global Internet. [6]

DNS enables human-friendly addressing, scalability, distributed resolution and efficient web access.

32. Discuss risks if DNS servers are attacked. [4]

Websites become inaccessible, phishing risk increases, service disruption occurs.

33. Define latency. [2]

Delay between request and response.

34. Explain how latency affects streaming. [3]

High latency causes delays and buffering interruptions.

35. What is Quality of Service (QoS)? [2]

Network mechanism to prioritise certain types of traffic.

36. Explain how QoS improves streaming. [3]

Prioritises video packets over less critical traffic.

37. Compare downloading and streaming. [4]

Downloading requires full file before playback; streaming plays while downloading.

38. Compare live and on-demand streaming. [3]

Live is real-time broadcast; on-demand is pre-recorded content.

39. Discuss advantages of streaming over traditional broadcasting. [6]

User control, global access, adaptive quality, cost efficiency.

40. Evaluate importance of CDNs in modern Internet. [6]

CDNs reduce latency, handle traffic spikes and improve reliability.

41–50. Create 10 additional scenario-based questions for classroom discussion and exam preparation.

Students should create DNS, URL, and streaming scenarios incorporating latency, buffering, caching, security and infrastructure considerations.

Exam-Style Questions

Describe measures needed to ensure video does not pause when streamed. [6]

High bandwidth, buffering, adaptive bitrate streaming, CDNs, low latency connection.
